\section*{Theory}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Theory}
In digital signal processing, several basic discrete-time signals serve as the building blocks for analyzing and synthesizing more complex signals~\cite{oppenheim_dsp}. The three most important among these are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Unit Impulse Signal ($\delta[n]$):} \\
          The unit impulse signal, $\delta[n]$, is defined as:
          \[
              \delta[n] =
              \begin{cases}
                  1, & n = 0    \\
                  0, & n \neq 0
              \end{cases}
          \]
          \begin{itemize}
              \item Serves as the identity element in convolution operations~\cite{oppenheim_dsp}.
              \item Has a value of 1 only at $n=0$ and 0 elsewhere.
              \item Commonly used to determine a system's impulse response.
          \end{itemize}

          \begin{figure}[H]
              \centering
              \includegraphics[width=0.6\textwidth]{impulse.png}
              \caption{Unit Impulse Signal $u[n]$}
          \end{figure}


    \item \textbf{Unit Step Signal ($u[n]$):} \\
          The unit step signal, $u[n]$, is defined as:
          \[
              u[n] =
              \begin{cases}
                  1, & n \geq 0 \\
                  0, & n < 0
              \end{cases}
          \]
          \begin{itemize}
              \item Switches from 0 to 1 at $n=0$ and remains at 1 for all subsequent $n$.
              \item Can be interpreted as the running sum of the impulse signal: $u[n] = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{n} \delta[k]$~\cite{oppenheim_dsp}.
          \end{itemize}

          \begin{figure}[H]
              \centering
              \includegraphics[width=0.6\textwidth]{Step.png}
              \caption{Unit Step Signal $u[n]$}
          \end{figure}

    \item \textbf{Unit Ramp Signal ($r[n]$):} \\
          The unit ramp signal, $r[n]$, is defined as:
          \[
              r[n] =
              \begin{cases}
                  0, & n < 0    \\
                  n, & n \geq 0
              \end{cases}
          \]
          \begin{itemize}
              \item Increases linearly with $n$ starting from zero.
              \item Represents the cumulative sum (discrete integration) of the unit step signal: $r[n] = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{n} u[k]$~\cite{oppenheim_dsp}.
          \end{itemize}

          \begin{figure}[H]
              \centering
              \includegraphics[width=0.6\textwidth]{ramp.png}
              \caption{Ramp Signal $u[n]$}
          \end{figure}
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Discussion}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Discussion}
In this experiment, we generated and transformed basic discrete-time signals—specifically the impulse, step, and ramp signals—using MATLAB. The “cumsum” function~\cite{matlab_cumsum} was essential for computing the cumulative sum, enabling us to convert a step signal into a ramp signal and an impulse signal into a step signal, thereby emulating discrete-time integration. Conversely, the “diff” function~\cite{matlab_diff} was used to perform discrete differentiation, allowing us to derive the step signal from the ramp and the impulse signal from the step. The resulting plots from these operations closely aligned with theoretical expectations, validating the correct implementation of these signal relationships. These hands-on conversions provided valuable insight into the mathematical links between the signals and reinforced our understanding of their interdependence.

\section*{Conclusion}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Conclusion}
The experiment effectively illustrated the generation and transformation of fundamental discrete-time signals using essential MATLAB functions. Through examining the characteristics and interconnections of the impulse, step, and ramp signals, we obtained practical understanding of their significance in digital signal processing. Employing functions such as \texttt{cumsum} and \texttt{diff} enabled us to model discrete-time integration and differentiation, respectively, and confirm theoretical principles via graphical results.
